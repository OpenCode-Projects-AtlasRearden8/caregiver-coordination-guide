# The First-Calls Checklist

When someone's care changes suddenly, the first week is a blur of phone calls. This checklist
put them in order so you spend less energy guessing who to call next. Work it top to bottom;
skip anything that does not apply to your family.

> **Coordination help, never medical advice.** This checklist covers logistics only. Emergency
> symptoms go to 911 (or your local emergency number), not to these calls.

## Before you start (five minutes)

Gather these so every call goes faster: the person's **full legal name**, **date of birth**,
**insurance/Medicare card**, and the **document grab-list** from
[the next chapter](02-document-grab-list.md) if you have it yet. Keep a pen next to the phone,
or open a notes app, and record **who you spoke to, on what date, and what they said**. That
note habit is the single most valuable thing in this chapter.

## The calls, in order

### 1. Their primary doctor's office

- Tell them your name, your relationship, and ask how to be listed as an emergency contact
  or authorized on the account.
- Ask: who covers after hours? Is there a nurse line? What is the fax or portal number for
  outside records?
- Read back what you heard. Off-script summary lines get heard correctly about twice as often
  as implied ones.

### 2. The pharmacy

- Confirm which pharmacy is on file and whether you may pick up for them
  (you usually need to be added as an authorized pickup person).
- Ask the pharmacist to run a full medication review conversation when you are ready — this is
  a **coordination question about their records**, not a medical question, and pharmacists handle
  these daily.

### 3. Insurance (Medicare / Medicaid / private plan)

- Ask what plan and plan number they have on file.
- Ask how prior authorizations work and how long they take.
- Ask for the patient-advocacy or case-management line. This is usually a different number
  than customer service, and it is staffed by people whose job is to untangle paperwork.

### 4. The hospital or facility itself

- Get the **case manager / social worker** assigned to your loved one's unit. Say the phrase:
  *"I'm coordinating care at home — can you connect me with the case manager?"* That sentence
  unlocks discharge planning, equipment lists, and referral paperwork.
- Discharge planning starts **on day one**, not on discharge day. Ask what the expected
  timeline is.

### 5. The local Area Agency on Aging (or equivalent in your region)

- In the U.S., the Eldercare Locator (1-800-677-1116) routes you to your local agency. Write
  your local agency's name and number below.
- They can point you to home-help programs, transportation services, and benefits checks —
  usually for free. You are not asking for favors; it is their function.

### 6. Home safety basics

- Ask the case manager whether a **home safety visit** or an occupational-therapy home
  assessment is offered before discharge.
- Note loose rugs, dark stairs, and the path from bed to bathroom. Cheap fixes (nightlights,
  grab bars, removing cords) prevent the most common injuries. Decisions about **which**
  equipment the person medically needs belong to their care team.

### 7. Employers and leave rights

- If you work, look up your employer's leave policy and, in the U.S., whether FMLA applies
  to your situation. HR can tell you what documentation they require — often the simplest
  form to get from a doctor's office.

### 8. Utilities and mail (only if they live far from you)

- If bills may be paid late during an emergency, call the utility and ask about a hard
  turn-off hold and payment-plan options. Put the person (not you) in the account name if
  you are only an authorized speaker: *"I am calling on behalf of the account holder."*

### 9. Their friends, minister, or neighbors

- The person who checks the mailbox knows your loved one's baseline. Ask them to call you
  if anything seems off. Offer to share your phone number with one trusted neighbor.

### 10. Yourself

- Write down today's date, your role, and what you have agreed to so far. Start the joint
  [sibling task-split worksheet](05-sibling-task-split.md) this week, before patterns harden.

## After the calls

- File call notes with the papers from the [Document Grab-List](02-document-grab-list.md).
- Put the recurring appointments (follow-ups) into one calendar you actually check.
- Print the [med log worksheet](03-med-logs.md) and the [doctor log](04-doctor-logs.md) and
  bring them to the first appointment.
