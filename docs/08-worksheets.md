# Worksheets

All the blank, printable templates live in one place. Print several copies of each: logs
work best on a clipboard near the meds, one new page per week. Fill them by hand first —
digital versions come easily later, and there is no "lost my password" accidentally-invented
featureset to fight here.

> **Coordination help, never medical advice.** These templates organize the paperwork of
> care. They never contain medical judgment. Columns say **what happened**; they never say
> what to do about anyone's health.

## How to fill them (one page of advice worth the whole chapter)

- **Pencil, not pen.** Plans change; graph paper forgives.
- **The day you learn it, write it.** A log written on Friday about two o'clock Tuesday is
  fiction; written Tuesday at two o'clock, it is evidence.
- **One fact per box.** Whole sentences in spreadsheets are why spreadsheets lose to
  clipboards.
- **Dates fully:** 2026-03-08, not "Tuesday." Locally, "3/8" may mean anything a century
  from now's archivist.
- **Blank boxes are data:** one empty check-off box on Wednesday means the dose was
  *uncertain*, which is different from *late* (write "late") or *skipped* (draw one slash).
  Three different words for three different phone calls to the prescriber.

## Printing tips

- Every worksheet page of this guide is print-friendly on purpose: black text, generous
  lines, no color dependencies. Legal pads of columns will also work in a pinch if you run
  out of printer paper before the pharmacy closes.
- Print the [med log](worksheets/med-log.md) and [daily check-off](worksheets/med-checkoff.md)
  as a stack — refills arrive faster than toner.
- Copy the same templates into a notes app if you prefer digital; the formats deliberately
  stay five columns or fewer so they survive transcription. **One master version wins:**
  digital or paper, not both halfway.

## Which pages come from where

Every worksheet ships as source in this repository's `worksheets/` folder and lands in the
built site alongside the chapters. The README shows the one-command build and
tests — no internet, no accounts, no data leaves your machine.

*All worksheets organize logistics and records. Nothing on them decides anything medical:
the care team holds that, and your job — the most important one — is the record.*
