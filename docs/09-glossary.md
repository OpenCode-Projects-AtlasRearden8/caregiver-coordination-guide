# Glossary

Plain-English translations of the words hospital staff, insurers, and agencies use. Filed
alphabetically. Remember the golden rule: **when a word confuses you, ask the person using
it to define it.** Nobody is ever offended by "what does that mean for us?"

> **Coordination help, never medical advice.** Definitions here explain logistics and
> paperwork words. Nothing here explains anyone's diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment —
> those explanations come from the care team.

| Term | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| **Activities of daily living (ADLs)** | The daily basics: bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, moving around. Staff use this term to figure out how much help someone needs. |
| **Advance directive** | A signed paper (made ahead of time) recording what medical care a person **does and does not** want if they cannot speak for themselves. |
| **Area Agency on Aging (AAA)** | A local public or nonprofit office that helps older adults and their families find services. Free calls; start with [your first calls](01-first-calls-checklist.md). |
| **Authorized representative** | Someone an agency officially recognizes as allowed to act or receive information for a person — different agencies have their own forms. |
| **Care plan** | The written summary of what help a person needs, who provides it, and how often. Hospitals, home agencies, and facilities each write one. |
| **Case manager / social worker** | The hospital or agency staff who arrange the logistics of leaving a facility: equipment, services, referrals. Say the words "case manager" early and often. |
| **Copay / coinsurance / deductible** | Your share of a bill, where the *amount* depends on plan details. Your insurer or plan's paperwork explains what applies — insurers answer these questions by phone daily. |
| **DNR (Do Not Resuscitate)** | A medical order, signed by a prescriber, that directs care teams regarding resuscitation attempts. It is a **clinical document**; whether one exists and what it says belongs to the person and their care team. |
| **Eldercare Locator** | The U.S. phone (1-800-677-1116) and web service that routes you to your Area Agency on Aging. |
| **FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act, U.S.)** | A U.S. law that can protect certain employees' jobs during qualifying family care leaves (unpaid). HR explains how it applies to a specific job; the U.S. Department of Labor explains the law. |
| **Geriatric care manager (aging life care professional)** | A professional — often a social worker or nurse by training — you can hire to assess needs and coordinate services. Useful when family lives far away; hire late in life as needed. |
| **HIPAA (U.S.)** | The U.S. privacy law around health information. Its practical meaning for you: staff will tell you **why** they cannot share information with you, and there are legitimate forms (like being named a personal representative, or the person telling staff it's OK) that can change that. Ask: "what form do we complete?" |
| **Home health care** | Skilled services (nursing, therapy) delivered at home, usually arranged by a doctor's order and often time-limited. |
| **Hospice** | A model of care focused on comfort when a physician has certified an eligibility window under a program's rules. Deciding about hospice is a medical and family conversation with clinicians — not something you DIY from a glossary. |
| **Medigap** | Private insurance that pays some costs that original Medicare does not. |
| **Non-medical / custodial home care** | Help at home that is not skilled nursing: meals, bathing help, companionship. Usually paid privately or by specific programs; agencies quote rates over the phone. |
| **POLST / MOLST / portable medical order** | A signed medical order form (names vary by state) recording treatments a person wants during an emergency, print-ready for paramedics and facilities. |
| **Power of attorney (POA)** | A signed document naming someone to act for a person — health care, finances, or both, depending on the document. Financial POA usually requires banks' own copies of the exact document. |
| **Prior authorization** | Insurer permission required **before** some services or drugs are covered. Ask early how long it takes. |
| **Respite care** | Short-term substitute care that gives the caregiver (you) a planned rest. See [Respite and Guilt](07-respite-and-guilt.md). |
| **Skilled nursing facility (SNF)** | A facility providing 24-hour licensed nursing care, often after a hospital stay, often with time-limited insurance coverage rules. The hospital case manager and insurer explain what applies. |
| **TBI / stroke rehab and "levels of care"** | Different settings (home, therapy hours per week, facility types) exist along a continuum. Which level is appropriate is decided by the medical team and the person — your role is logistics and questions. |

## Ask-this-script for any confusing word

> "That word is new to me. Can you tell me what it means in our situation, and where I can
> read about it in writing?"

Ask it early in conversations, write the answer in your [doctor log](04-doctor-logs.md),
and add any new recurring terms to this glossary — that is what it is for.

*This glossary defines paperwork and logistics vocabulary. It is coordination help, never
medical advice.*
