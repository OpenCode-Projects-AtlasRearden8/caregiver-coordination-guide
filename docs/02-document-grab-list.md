# The Document Grab-List

Papers are leverage. With the right documents in hand, you can make appointments, fill
refills, file insurance claims, and get help in days instead of weeks. This chapter is a
treasure map: **what to find, where it usually hides, and what to do if it is missing.**

> **Coordination help, never medical advice.** Nothing here is legal, tax, or medical advice.
> For wills, powers of attorney, or benefits decisions, consult a qualified professional —
> your local Area Agency on Aging can usually point you to low-cost legal help first.

## The three-day emergency pouch

These seven items go in a folder (or a phone photo) you can grab in ten seconds during an ER
trip. Everything else can wait.

1. Photo ID for the person.
2. Insurance / Medicare card (front and back).
3. Current medication list (start the [med log](03-med-logs.md) today — an approximate list
   beats no list).
4. Allergies and major conditions, written in one sentence each.
5. Emergency contacts, with the phone number that actually reaches a human.
6. Advance directive or living will, **if one exists** (see below if it does not).
7. Doctors list: names, practices, phone numbers — start the [doctor log](04-doctor-logs.md).

## The full grab-list

Work down the list in short sessions. Put a check only when you have **touched the real
document** — hearing about it does not count.

- [ ] Government IDs: driver's license or state ID, Social Security card, birth certificate
- [ ] Passport (if they have one)
- [ ] Military discharge papers (DD-214), if applicable
- [ ] Medicare / Medicaid cards and any supplemental (" Medigap ") policies
- [ ] Private insurance cards, including dental and vision
- [ ] Prescription drug plan details
- [ ] Health-care **Power of Attorney** / health-care proxy — the signed original
- [ ] Financial Power of Attorney — the signed original, plus any bank's "acceptance" copy
- [ ] Living will / advance directive / POLST or similar portable medical order form
- [ ] DNR order, if one exists (paramedics ask for the paper or the bracelet)
- [ ] A list of all doctors, dentists, and therapists they see
- [ ] Last two years of tax returns and property-tax notices
- [ ] Pension statements, Social Security award letter, VA benefit letters
- [ ] Mortgage statement or lease, homeowners/renters insurance
- [ ] Deeds, vehicle titles, loan statements
- [ ] Bank and credit-union account statements (one recent statement each)
- [ ] Investment or retirement account statements
- [ ] Credit cards, (stolen-card phone numbers stay with the issuer — never store card numbers loose)
- [ ] Utility accounts: electric, gas, water, phone, internet
- [ ] Life insurance policies and annuities
- [ ] Funeral or burial plans, deeds to plots, prepaid arrangements
- [ ] Safe deposit box: location, key, and who is listed on the signature card
- [ ] Passwords are best stored in a legal **password manager** or with your attorney's
      records — never in a plain text file or on a sticky note

## Where these papers usually hide

| Item | Usual hiding spots |
| --- | --- |
| Insurance cards | Wallet, glovebox, kitchen drawer (often duplicate in "the good drawer") |
| Advance directive | With their lawyer, in the safe deposit box, or in a filing cabinet "A–F" folder |
| Pension / VA papers | A shoebox of mail from the year they retired |
| Marriage / birth certificates | Home safe, cedar chest, or in the same envelope as the will |
| Utility bills | A stack on the kitchen counter or a "to pay" tray |
| The will | Attorney's office (call the number on old letterhead) or the safe deposit box |

## Replacement steps, if a document is gone

- **Social Security card:** request a replacement through the Social Security Administration.
- **Medicare card:** call 1-800-MEDICARE or redo it on your Medicare account online.
- **Birth certificate:** from the Vital Records office in the state where they were born.
- **ID / driver's license:** the DMV; many states offer a homebound replacement process.
- **Advance directive:** does not need a lawyer; most states' forms are free. If the person
  can still sign forms, doing one now is a gift to the whole family. **Do not** guess at
  medical forms for someone else — the person signs it, or their legally appointed agent does.
- **Power of Attorney:** if none exists and the person cannot sign new documents, that is a
  guardianship question for an elder-law attorney — time-sensitive, so ask early.

## Where to keep the copies (and who gets them)

Keep **one original set** where the person (or the legal agent) says is correct, and **one
"working set"** with the person coordinating care. Copies of key items also go with:

- The emergency pouch above
- One sibling (so a house fire or a falling-out does not take out the only copy)
- A sealed envelope labeled "open only if" with the person's own instructions — if they are
  able to give any. Their rules outrank convenience.

Photographing documents is fine for your own working set; store photos in a password manager
or an encrypted device, **not** in a shared group chat and never in email drafts.
