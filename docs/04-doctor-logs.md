# Medical Logs: Doctors

Appointments with doctors are where caregiving paperwork either becomes a system or becomes
a shoebox. This chapter is the doctor log: a simple record of **who said what, when, and what
happens next**, kept so you never rely on memory at the exact moment it fails.

> **Coordination help, never medical advice.** This chapter teaches you how to organize visits
> and record information. What a symptom means, which treatment is right, and every clinical
> decision belongs to your loved one and their care team. Your job is the calendar, the paper
> trail, and the room's working memory.

## Bring three things to every visit

1. The **med log** (see [Medical Logs: Meds](03-med-logs.md)) — current version, plus anything
   new since last visit.
2. The **doctor log page** from this chapter — so the previous plan of care and their open
   questions are on the desk.
3. **One written list of questions**, maximum five, in the order you care about. Five written
   beats twelve remembered-fuzzy.

## The one-sentence-per-answer rule

During the visit, when the doctor answers a question, write down **one sentence, their words
as close as you can get them**, under the question. Do not transcribe. Do not interpret. If
you need clarification on an instruction, ask now while the chart is open: *"Just so I write
this correctly — is it twice a day, or morning only?"* Doctors respect that sentence.

If an adult family member is present, split roles: one listens, one writes. You cannot do
both well, especially under stress.

## The non-negotiable bottom line of every visit

Before leaving the room, every single time, ask these three logistical questions:

1. **"What happens next, and who does it?"** (referral placed? test ordered? follow-up call?)
2. **"When should we expect results, and who calls whom?"**
3. **"If something changes at home, do we call this office, the nurse line, or the ER?"**

Write down the answers under their matching questions in the log. These three lines prevent
the classic *"we're still waiting on the referral we never received"* month.

## What the columns of the worksheet are for

- **Date / Doctor / Reason** — exactly what is on the appointment card.
- **Questions & answers** — your list from before the visit, plus one-sentence answers.
- **What was changed** — new or stopped medications (mirror it into the [med log](03-med-logs.md)
  the same day), new referrals, tests ordered.
- **Next steps & by whom** — question 1's answer, with names.
- **Follow-up date** — the actual calendar date, not "in two weeks."

## Use the patient portal (or ask how to)

If the practice has a portal, ask the front desk to get you signed up during the visit, and
ask *whether* you may receive notices under your own access or as their authorized
representative — the rules vary by practice and state, so this is a "how does **this**
office handle it" question, not a general rule. Portal messages are searchable history;
paper notes are the backup when portals are down or the gramophone is the aesthetic.

## Between visits: the symptoms notebook

Keep one spiral notebook (or note on your phone) where you jot, dated and time-stamped, any
symptom, fall, chemically strange day, or new complaint. You are not diagnosing — you are
**collecting dates**. At the next visit, "increased confusion for ten days now, started on
the twelfth" is worth five pages of anecdote in the answer line.

Ask the care team how little symptoms should be reported *in between* visits once, and follow
their instructions exactly — this is the same "ask the professionals" posture from every other
page of this guide.

## After major visits

- Same day: update the [med log](03-med-logs.md) with any drug changes.
- Same day: put the follow-up date in the family calendar, mention it in your
  [weekly update](06-ai-weekly-updates.md).
- Same day: text the sibling group the **headline only** ("cardiology moved to the 14th, all
  values stable") — details live in the letter, not the chat.
- Never share medical details or screenshots in group chats. Personal health information
  belongs in person, by phone, or on paper you controlled.

*This chapter is calendar and notebook strategy. It is coordination help, never medical
advice — the clinicians interpret anything physical or clinical.*
